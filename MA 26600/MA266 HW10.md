$$\mathbf{\frac{dx}{dt}=x^2(x^2-4)}$$
$$\int  \frac{dx}{x^2(x^2-4)}=\int  dt=t+C$$
$$\frac{1}{x^2(x^2-4)}=\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x^2}+\frac{C}{x-2}+\frac{D}{x+2}$$
$$1=Ax(x^2-4)+B(x^2-4)+Cx^2(x+2)+Dx^2(x-2)$$
$$\text{By choosing }x=0,2,-2\text{ we get } \frac{1}{x^2(x^2-4)}=\frac{1}{16(x-2)}-\frac{1}{4x^2}-\frac{1}{16(x+2)}$$
$$\int  \left(  \frac{1}{16(x-2)}-\frac{1}{4x^2}-\frac{1}{16(x+2)}\right)dx=\frac{1}{4x}+\frac{1}{16}\ln \left| \frac{x-2}{x+2} \right|=t+C$$
$$~~$$
$$\text{As seen in the graph, }x=0 \text{ is semistable},~x=-2\text{ is stable},~x=2\text{ is unstable}$$
