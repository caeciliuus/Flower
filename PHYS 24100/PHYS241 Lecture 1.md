# Maxwell's Equations
## Differential form
 $div(\vec{E})=\vec{\nabla}\cdot \vec{E}=\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{0}}$
 $div(\vec{B})=\vec{\nabla}\cdot \vec{B}=0$
 $curl(\vec{E})=\vec{\nabla}\times \vec{E}=-\frac{\partial\vec{B}}{\partial t}$
$curl(\vec{B})=\vec{\nabla}\times \vec{B}=\mu_{0}\left[ \vec{J}+\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \right]$
## Integral form
$\oint_{S} \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{A}=\frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_{0}}$
$\oint_{S}\vec{B}\cdot d\vec{A}=0$
$\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l}=-\oint \frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}\cdot d\vec{A}$
$\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l}=-\mu_{0}I+\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \int _S \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}\cdot d\vec{A}$
# Electric charge & Coulomb's Law
- Electric charge: intrinsic characteristic of the fundamental particles that make up objects
	- Electrically neutral: object contains equal parts of (+) and (-) charges
	- Net charge: imbalance in charge
- Electric charge is quantized: $q=ne$ where $n=\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\dots$
- Elementary charge: $e=1.60217662(63)E-19$ C
	- One coulomb (C) is the amount of charge that is transferred through the cross section of a wire in 1 second when there's a current of 1 ampere in the wire
- Electro-static force of attraction/repulsion has a magnitude $k \frac{Q_{1}Q_{2}}{r^2}$ where $k=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}}=8.99E9 Nm^2/C$
	- Coulomb's Law: $\vec{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}} \frac{Q_{1}Q_{2}}{r^2}\hat{r}$
# Conductors & Insulators
- Conductors: material in which electric charge can move around "freely"
- Insulators: material in which electric charges are "frozen" in place
- Semi-conductor: material in which electric charges can move around but not as freely as conductors
- Super-conductor: no resistance to the movement of charge
