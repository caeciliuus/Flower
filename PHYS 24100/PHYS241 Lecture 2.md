- Scalar fields have only magnitude, vector fields have magnitude and direction
- Use "test charge" to determine direction of electric field
- Electric field $\vec{E}=\frac{\vec{F}}{q_{0}}$, exists at direction of force, units given by $N/C$
$$\vec{F}=\frac{q_{0}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}}\left( \frac{q_{1}}{r_{1}^2}\hat{r}_{1}+\frac{q_{2}}{r_{2}^2}\hat{r}_{2} \right)$$
![[Pasted image 20250828155841.png|right|350]]
- To find resultant force from a point charge, $\vec{F}=\vec{F}_{1}+\vec{F}_{2}+\dots+\vec{F}_{n}$
- Electric field obeys principle of superposition -> the field of a system of charged objects is equal to [vector] sum of the field in each individual charged object in the system
- Electric field lines originate from positive charges and terminates at negative charges
- Direction of electric field at any location is tangential to the field line there
- $|\vec{E}|$ at any location is $\propto$ $p_{lines}$ there
- Electric dipole: opposite signs but equal magnitude

> [!exm]   
> ![[Pasted image 20250828160636.png|right|150]]
>
> $$|\vec{E}|=|E_{+}+E_{-},r_{+}=z-\frac{d}{2},r_{-}=z+\frac{d}{2}$$
> $$|\vec{E}|=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}}\left[ \frac{q}{\left( z-\frac{d}{2} \right)^2} - \frac{q}{\left( z+\frac{d}{2} \right)^2}\right]\leftarrow(\text{binomial expansion})\rightarrow \frac{qd}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}z^3}$$

![[Pasted image 20250828160733.png|right|400]]
- Electric dipole moment $\vec{p}=q\vec{d}$, given in $C-m$
	- Rewritten $|\vec{E}|$ at P: $\vec{E}=\frac{\vec{p}}{2\pi\varepsilon_{0}z^3}$
- If you put a dipole in a uniform electric field, it'll experience a torque, $\vec{\tau}=\vec{p}\times \vec{E}=-pE\sin \theta$, as the dipole moment attempts to correct distance $\parallel$ to $\vec{E}$
- $\vec{F}=m\vec{a}=q\vec{E}$ -> acceleration given by $a=\frac{q}{m}\vec{E}$
